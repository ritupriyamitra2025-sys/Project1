Litigation Automation Project — Project Understanding

Version: 1 (7 October 2026) · Source: 01-Original-Instructions/2026-10-07_U-1_Project-Plan_ORIGINAL.docx · Status: planning only; not started.

  The owner’s folder names below are suggestions. The AI may change the structure, names and sub-folders if it serves the end goal better. Any change must be recorded in 03-Instruction-Log.md and in this document.

1. End goal

The owner places any new court paper into the project. Examples are an order from any court, a filing by the other party, or the owner’s own court-stamped filing. The project then does the needed work with very little further instruction. It:

1.  Saves and numbers the paper, and makes a same-name text file.
2.  Sorts it by court and by type: procedural, merits, or both.
3.  Links it into the history of that issue (trackback).
4.  Works out the next step and the due date under the court’s rules.
5.  Prepares the next filing as Word and PDF, with record cross-references and the cited case law.
6.  Tests the filing as the opposing top attorney and as the judge would (devil’s advocate), and fixes the weak points.
7.  Delivers a README: what to file, which court, by what date, what to attach — “your file is ready”.

The project must run under any AI (Claude, Cursor, ChatGPT and others). It uses relative paths so the whole folder can move between drives.

Courts: any USA court (trial, appeal, supreme), plus Canada and India, ready for when cases arise there.

2. Workflow (one new paper)

    1  Inbox (raw paper)
    → 2  Source library (numbered, + .txt)
    → 3  Procedural / Merits (both if mixed)
    → 4  Trackback chain for the issue (+ 2 zips)
    → 11 Orders index (if it is an order)
    → 6  Rules: next step and deadline (rule number + source)
    → 12 Preparation folder for the due date (DOCX + PDF, record refs, case law)
    → 10 Devil's advocate review → corrections → review points closed
    → README "ready to file"

If the owner files a motion, folder 10 also predicts the opponent’s likely response and a likely order. When the real response arrives, the prepared reply is fine-tuned.

5. Safeguards (legal accuracy)

-   Every deadline is tied to a rule number and source, plus the date it was checked. The owner confirms deadlines before relying on them.
-   Every case citation is verified against a real database (for example CourtListener) before it goes into a filing. AI can invent cases.
-   Scanned PDFs: convert to text (OCR) and mark pages that may contain errors.
-   No papers are sealed or confidential (owner, 2026-10-07).
